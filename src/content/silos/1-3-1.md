## What you receive

You receive a complete CPSR for each product, written to the structure EU law sets out in Annex I of Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009:

- Part A, the cosmetic product safety information, compiled from your data and your suppliers' data and organized under the Annex I headings.
- Part B, the cosmetic product safety assessment, with its conclusion, the labelled warnings and instructions of use, and the reasoning that links Part A to the conclusion.
- Support with the product information file (PIF) that the report sits in.

Every conclusion is referenced to its source, so your team can answer an authority's question without rebuilding the argument. The report comes in English or Portuguese. You get a reviewed final document and support for questions about what we delivered.

One check is worth making on any Part B, from us or anyone else. Annex I requires it to carry the safety assessor's name and address, proof of qualification, and the date and signature. If one of those four is missing, the report is incomplete.

Not sure how the two parts work, or who has to hold the report? Our guide to the [Cosmetic Product Safety Report (CPSR)](/services/toxicological-risk-assessment/cpsr/) covers the legal side.

## What we need from you to start

You don't need any of this for the first conversation. The list below is what a CPSR draws on once the project starts, and it all goes through an NDA first. It follows the Part A headings of Annex I and the Commission's guidelines on that annex.

- The full formula, with the quantity of each ingredient, its INCI name, CAS number where available and its function.
- Fragrance information: the name and code number of the fragrance compound and its supplier. The guidelines say information on the regulated substances it contains, and anything else relevant to safety, should be disclosed to the responsible person and the safety assessor.
- Raw material data from your suppliers: specifications, purity and impurity data. The guidelines say supplier data should be preferred for impurities and packaging.
- Physical and chemical data and stability results for the finished product, under reasonably foreseeable storage conditions.
- Microbiological data: specifications and the preservation challenge test results. Some low-risk products don't need a challenge test, but the reason has to be justified, and we'll tell you if that applies.
- Packaging information, in particular the purity and stability of the material in contact with the product.
- How the product is used: the label text, warnings and instructions, where it's applied, how much, how often and by whom.
- Undesirable effects data, if the product, or a similar one, is already on the market.
- Any existing studies, such as tests in human volunteers.

### When something is missing

We'd rather find a gap at the start than at the end. After you share your documents under NDA, we confirm what arrived and send a list of anything missing. If the gap is in eye irritation, skin irritation or skin sensitization, we can design an animal-free testing strategy based on OECD guidelines, including the choice of CRO and the reading of the reports. We don't run laboratory tests ourselves; partner laboratories or the CRO of your choice do.

## How the process works

The path from your first message to a written proposal has six steps.

1. **You send a short message.** Tell us about the product and the target market. No confidential data is needed yet.
2. **We reply to schedule.** We read your request and come back with times for a diagnostic meeting.
3. **NDA first, if you need it.** If you want to discuss product data in that meeting, we sign a mutual NDA before it.
4. **Diagnostic meeting, at no cost.** A video call about your product, target market, existing data and timeline. You leave with a first reading: whether the case fits, what kind of project makes sense and which data we need to scope it.
5. **Data for scoping, under NDA.** If needed, you share the documents that let us size the work.
6. **A written proposal.** Scope, deliverables, timeline, fee and engagement model, with the assumptions and what is out of scope.

Once you approve the proposal, the work runs with agreed checkpoints and direct communication with the specialist writing your report. The full description is on our [how we work](/how-we-work/) page.

## What shapes the timeline and the fee

We don't quote a fixed price or turnaround before we've seen the case. These are the factors that set both:

- The size of the question: one product, or several products in the same project.
- The amount and quality of your existing data: a complete supplier file with stability and challenge test results already done, or gaps that need searching, analysis or new tests before we can conclude.
- The target agencies: the EU alone, or the EU plus ANVISA documentation for Brazil.
- The deliverable: a full CPSR, or support on part of an existing PIF.
- Your schedule: a regular timeline, or an urgent launch date.
- The engagement model: a fixed-scope project, or a technical review of a report someone else prepared.
