## What a toxicological risk assessment answers

A toxicological risk assessment asks one question: can this substance or product harm people at the dose they will actually receive? Hazard alone doesn't settle it. An ingredient that irritates skin at high concentration can be acceptable at the level found in a finished product, used the way the label says.

So the assessment connects two sets of facts. One is what the substance can do to the body. The other is how much of it reaches the body, by which route and how often.

The Scientific Committee on Consumer Safety (SCCS) gives a clear map of that work. It's the committee that issues opinions to the European Commission on the safety of non-food consumer products, cosmetics included. Its Notes of Guidance describe a process "universally applied for chemical substances," in four steps. Each step answers its own question:

1. **Hazard identification.** What are the intrinsic toxic properties of the substance? Evidence can come from in vivo studies, in vitro and ex vivo tests, in chemico methods, in silico methods and read-across, clinical studies, case reports, epidemiology and post-market data.
2. **Exposure assessment.** How much of the substance reaches people? The answer depends on its function, its concentration in each product and how often the product is used.
3. **Dose-response assessment.** Where does an effect begin? This step sets a point of departure, such as a no observed adverse effect level (NOAEL) or a benchmark dose.
4. **Risk characterization.** How far is the expected exposure from that point of departure? For threshold effects, the SCCS expresses the distance as a margin of safety: the systemic point of departure divided by the systemic exposure dose.

## What we assess

Our toxicological assessment work covers four kinds of subject. Each one starts from a critical review of the literature and of the regulatory requirements that apply to it.

- Substances and ingredients: a toxicological profile that gathers and weighs what is known about a molecule or raw material, endpoint by endpoint.
- Medicines and molecules: a safety assessment based on the published and internal data available for the substance.
- Finished products: an assessment of the formulated product as a whole, including how it's used and by whom.
- Cosmetics: the safety report that EU law requires before a product reaches the market, plus support with the product information file.

For the EU cosmetics market, that work centers on the [Cosmetic Product Safety Report (CPSR)](/services/toxicological-risk-assessment/cpsr/), which has a page of its own. For Brazil, we prepare the documentation for submissions to ANVISA, the Brazilian Health Regulatory Agency.

## How we build an assessment

### Existing data come first

We start with what's already there: published literature, public databases and your internal data. Each piece of evidence is checked for quality and relevance before it carries weight in a conclusion. EU cosmetics law asks for the same discipline. Article 10 of Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009 requires "an appropriate weight-of-evidence approach" for reviewing data from all existing sources.

### Non-animal methods where they answer the question

We use in silico approaches, read-across and other new approach methodologies (NAMs) where they answer the regulatory question. Where they don't, we say so plainly and name the study that is still needed. In cosmetics the line is also legal: the SCCS states that only validated replacement methods, or methods shown to be scientifically valid, should be used when testing for the purposes of EU cosmetic legislation. Our [NAMs strategy](/nams-strategy/) page explains how we decide.

### Every conclusion points to its source

Each conclusion in our reports is referenced to the study, database or guideline behind it. Your team can answer a question from an agency or an auditor without rebuilding the argument from scratch.

### We interpret studies; partner labs run them

We don't run laboratory studies. When new data are needed, we design the study, guide the laboratory, oversee execution at the CRO and interpret the results. Partner laboratories or the CRO of your choice carry out the work.

## What you receive

The deliverable is a written document, with every conclusion referenced to its source. Depending on the project, it takes one of these forms:

- a toxicological profile of a substance or ingredient;
- a safety assessment of a finished product;
- a CPSR, Parts A and B, with support for the product information file;
- documentation for an ANVISA submission.

Documents are written in English or Portuguese, in the language of your team and of the target agency. You get a reviewed final document and support for questions about what we delivered.

## What sits outside this service

Clear scope saves time on both sides, so here is where this service stops.

- Hazard communication: GHS classification and Safety Data Sheets are a separate service. You'll find it with [all our services](/services/).
- Program strategy for drug candidates: Deciding which nonclinical studies to run, waive or reorder before first-in-human is strategy work, covered on the NAMs strategy page.
- The regulatory decision: We assess the case and build the scientific rationale. Whether an agency accepts it is the agency's decision, and we don't promise an outcome that isn't ours to give.
